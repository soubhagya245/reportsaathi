Final problem statement

Older Kannada-speaking patients in Bengaluru receive lab reports and prescriptions in English and reach the doctor without knowing what the results or medicines mean. They need a plain-language explanation in Kannada before the visit, but we have not yet confirmed how often this happens, who they rely on today, or what it costs them.

Proposed solution

Start with a family-helper tool (option 2), tested with a human-run version before any app is built.

What it is: the user (most likely the son or daughter) photographs a report or prescription. The tool returns a short explanation in simple Kannada or English, marks values that are outside the printed reference range, and writes 3 to 5 questions to ask the doctor. It never says “you are fine” or suggests treatment. It always sends the person back to the doctor.

Why this one:

It is far cheaper and safer than a voice-first app, because a literate family member can sanity-check the output.
It targets the gap you described: the time between getting the report and seeing the doctor.
It can be tested this week, with no software, by running it manually.

How to test it (2 to 3 weeks):

Find 10 older Kannada speakers or their families, through a lab, pharmacy, or neighbourhood.
Take their anonymized reports and produce the explanation yourself, using an AI chat plus a doctor’s review.
Send it by WhatsApp and ask: Did you read it? Did it change what you asked the doctor? Would you use it again? Would you pay?

Build only if most testers use it unprompted and say it changed the visit. If voice turns out to matter (the patient can’t read), add Kannada audio next. If nobody cares, a printed card (option 3) may be all that is needed.

Safety rules from day one:

A doctor reviews the explanation templates.
Disclaimer on every output, plus a clear “see your doctor” line.
Get consent before handling anyone’s health data, and delete images after use (India’s DPDP Act applies).
Have an Indian health-tech lawyer check the regulatory position before any public launch.

Biggest open assumption: that this problem is frequent and painful enough to matter. Interviews will settle that faster than building.
